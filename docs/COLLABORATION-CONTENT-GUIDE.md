# Collaboration Content & Editorial Style Guide (Phase 21)

## 1. Editorial Philosophy

The Collaboration System communicates with technical clarity, professional confidence, and restrained honesty. It speaks to engineers, researchers, and technical leaders who value substance over marketing hype.

---

## 2. Voice & Tone Rules

### 1. Problem-First vs. Technology-First
- **Do Not Say:** *"I offer full-stack development services in React, Python, Next.js, and PyTorch."*
- **Do Say:** *"I collaborate on systems where engineering rigor, mathematical modeling, and production stability matter—specifically quantitative time-series pipelines, multi-agent AI workflows, and high-performance web applications."*

### 2. First-Person Singular Identity
- **Do Not Say:** *"Our agency delivers cutting-edge digital transformation solutions for your business."*
- **Do Say:** *"I engage directly on specific technical challenges, working as a sole architect or embedded collaborator."*

### 3. Truthful Evidence Grounding
- **Do Not Say:** *"Recognized as one of the top AI engineers in Pakistan with hundreds of satisfied clients."*
- **Do Say:** *"Every collaboration area is backed by demonstrable public GitHub repositories, published research inquiries, and empirical lab benchmarks."*

---

## 3. Prohibited Marketing Clichés

The following phrases and structures are strictly banned across the collaboration system and enforced by `scripts/validate-collaboration.mjs`:
- `world-class`
- `rockstar`
- `10x engineer`
- `best developer in`
- `our services include`
- `pricing table`
- `cheap`
- `guaranteed ROI`
- `hire me now`
- Artificial urgency (*"Only 2 slots left this week"*)
- Fabricated testimonial cards or client logos

---

## 4. Availability Integrity
Availability must always be explicit, truthful, and date-stamped (`updatedAt: "YYYY-MM-DD"`). Stale availability states must never be masked.
